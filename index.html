Camera types explained

Solar CCTV Camera Types — What Each One Actually Does

Every meaningful category of solar-powered security camera is explained below. This is a capability guide, not a stock list — whether a specific brand or model is available can be confirmed on request before you commit to anything.

Important: “solar-powered” does not mean unlimited power

A solar camera runs on stored sunshine, so real-world performance depends on: battery capacity, panel wattage, direct sunlight and shade, recording mode, mobile signal strength, temperature, how often motion triggers the camera, and the product's design quality. A correctly sized system records reliably through normal conditions; an undersized one disappoints during cloudy spells. We size honestly using the estimator tools and the manufacturer's datasheet — never guesswork.

1. All-in-one: solar panel + battery + camera unit

One integrated product: the panel, battery, camera and electronics are built into a single housing. Mount it, pair it, done — no wiring between components. This is the simplest and most popular category for homes, shops and gates.

Best for
House entrances, shops, farm gates, small plots — anywhere one neat unit is enough.
Network
Wi-Fi or 4G SIM depending on the model — confirm before purchase.
Power reality
Panel and battery are fixed, so you cannot upgrade them later; check the datasheet values.
Storage
Usually microSD; cloud only if the model officially supports it.
Setup
Lowest of all types — mount, pair in app, test.
Failure points & what to ask before buying

Common issues: undersized battery for continuous recording, panel shaded by trees or dust, weak signal at the mount height.

Ask: What is the battery capacity (Wh) and panel wattage (W)? Does it record continuously or on motion? Is the panel replaceable if it fails? Which exact app does it use?

Ask about this type

2. Separate solar panel connected to a compatible camera

A battery camera paired with a separate, larger solar panel via a cable. The panel can be placed in full sun while the camera sits in shade — the right answer when the mounting spot itself is dark, and a way to add more charging capacity.

Best for
Shaded walls, north-facing spots (in India), verandahs, and battery cameras that drain fast.
Network
Depends on the camera — Wi-Fi or 4G.
Power reality
Better charging flexibility, but the panel and camera must be electrically compatible — voltage and connector must match the manufacturer's guidance.
Storage
Same options as the host camera (usually microSD).
Setup
Moderate — two items to mount, one cable run to protect.
Failure points & what to ask before buying

Common issues: wrong panel-to-camera compatibility, cable chewed by rodents, loose weather-exposed connectors.

Ask: Is this panel officially listed as compatible with this camera? What cable length is included? Is the connector weatherproof?

Ask about this type

3. 4G / LTE SIM-based solar camera — works without Wi-Fi

The camera carries its own SIM card and connects over the mobile network, exactly like a phone. This is the category that solves farms, pump houses, open plots and construction sites where no Wi-Fi exists. Live view and alerts work wherever your phone has internet.

Best for
Farms, fields, remote pumps, construction sites, layouts — any place without broadband.
Network
Needs adequate 4G signal at the mount point and a working data plan in the SIM.
Power reality
4G transmission uses more power than Wi-Fi — battery and panel need to be sized accordingly.
Storage
Typically microSD locally; some models add cloud — confirm per model.
Setup
Moderate — SIM activation, possible APN setting, signal check, app binding.
Failure points & what to ask before buying

Common issues: no network at the exact spot, SIM locked/blocked, data plan exhausted, unsupported operator or LTE band, APN mismatch.

Ask: Which operators and LTE bands does this camera support? What SIM size? Does it need APN settings? Is the SIM slot locked to one carrier?

Ask about this type

4. Wi-Fi solar camera — for sites with reliable coverage

Connects to your existing router over Wi-Fi, with the sun providing the power. No data plan is needed, and setup is usually the cheapest route — provided the Wi-Fi genuinely reaches the mounting point with a solid signal.

Best for
Homes, shops, schools, warehouses where the router covers the camera position.
Network
Requires a dependable Wi-Fi signal at the mount point — check band support (2.4 GHz / 5 GHz) in the manual.
Power reality
Lower radio power draw than 4G; same sunlight rules apply.
Storage
microSD usually; cloud if the model supports it.
Setup
Easy — app pairing with the router, then a signal test at the mounting spot.
Failure points & what to ask before buying

Common issues: Wi-Fi drops with distance/walls, router reboots leave camera offline, band mismatch (camera needs 2.4 GHz, router broadcasts 5 GHz only).

Ask: Which Wi-Fi bands does the camera support? What happens after a power cut at the router — does it reconnect by itself?

Ask about this type

5. Solar PTZ camera — pan, tilt, zoom (where supported)

A solar camera whose head rotates and tilts — you steer the view from the app and, on some models, zoom optically or digitally. One PTZ can watch a large yard from several angles, trading a wider reach for a narrower moment-by-moment view.

Best for
Large farm plots, construction sites, layouts, parking yards — places where one vantage point must cover a lot.
Network
Wi-Fi or 4G depending on model; PTZ motors and 4G radios both increase power draw.
Power reality
Highest power needs in the solar category — the datasheet values matter most here.
Storage
microSD and/or cloud as supported; auto-tracking models write more footage.
Setup
Moderate — same as fixed plus movement-range and tracking configuration.
Failure points & what to ask before buying

Common issues: faster battery drain, tracking false alerts from leaves/animals/rain, moving parts exposed to weather.

Ask: What is the pan/tilt range? Is zoom optical or digital? Is auto-tracking included and how does it affect battery and data use?

Ask about this type

6. Fixed-lens / fixed-view solar camera

One direction, one field of view, no moving parts. Fewer things can fail, power draw is lowest, and a well-aimed fixed camera covering a gate or door is often more useful than a distant PTZ.

Best for
Gates, doorways, counters, cash desks, store rooms, pump houses — defined single points.
Network
Wi-Fi or 4G per model.
Power reality
Most energy-efficient category — easiest to run reliably through cloudy spells.
Storage
microSD and/or cloud as supported.
Setup
Easiest — mount, aim, pair.
Failure points & what to ask before buying

Common issues: wrong aiming (too high, too far), lens fogging in humid weather, blind spots beside the view.

Ask: What is the field of view (degrees) and lens size? At what mounting height does the manual recommend installing it?

Ask about this type

7. Battery outdoor camera + solar charging accessory

A wire-free battery camera first, solar panel added to keep the battery topped up. This is how most “wireless solar” kits are actually built. The battery is the real power source; the panel extends it — so battery health decides reliability.

Best for
Rented premises and quick installs where drilling for power cables is not wanted.
Network
Wi-Fi or 4G per model.
Power reality
Motion-triggered operation by design; continuous 24/7 recording is usually not realistic on this architecture — verify per model.
Storage
microSD / cloud as supported.
Setup
Easy; plan the panel position for sun, not just the camera position.
Failure points & what to ask before buying

Common issues: battery ageing after 1–3 years depending on climate, cold mornings reducing capacity, panel undersized for frequent motion events.

Ask: What is the battery capacity and expected life? Is the solar panel included or an accessory? Can the battery be replaced?

Ask about this type

8. 4G camera with microSD and/or cloud storage (where supported)

Storage choice shapes monthly costs and what happens after a theft. Local microSD keeps footage inside the camera — cheap and private, but lost if the camera is stolen or damaged. Cloud (only where the model officially supports it) keeps copies off-site, usually with a subscription.

Best for
Anyone who must decide consciously between on-site and off-site evidence.
Network
Cloud features need dependable internet at the camera; microSD does not.
Power reality
Continuous uploads to cloud increase power and data consumption significantly.
Storage
Check supported card capacity and endurance class in the exact model's manual; use high-endurance cards for CCTV.
Setup
Card formatting, retention/overwrite settings, and (if used) cloud account configuration.
Failure points & what to ask before buying

Common issues: non-endurance cards failing after months of writes, fake/counterfeit cards corrupting footage, cloud fees recurring, camera theft taking the evidence.

Ask: What is the maximum supported card size? Does the maker recommend endurance-rated cards? What exactly does the cloud plan cost and include?

Ask about this type

9. AI / person / vehicle detection (where the exact model supports it)

Some models analyse the image and can distinguish people and vehicles from general movement. Done well, this cuts false alerts from leaves, birds, rain and stray animals — the single biggest complaint about cheap outdoor cameras. Detection quality varies enormously between models, so treat maker claims as things to verify, not promises.

Best for
Farms and sites where animals and wind trigger ordinary motion cameras constantly.
Network
Independent of network type — a camera feature.
Power reality
On-device analysis usually costs little power; cloud-based analysis costs data.
Storage
Smart alerts can mark clips, making playback review faster.
Setup
Enable detection type, set sensitivity, test with realistic movement.
Failure points & what to ask before buying

Common issues: “AI” that is really just basic motion detection, missed events at long distance, alerts delayed by weak signal.

Ask: Is person/vehicle detection performed on the device itself? Can alerts be limited to specific detection types and schedules? What do independent reviews say?

Ask about this type

10. Night vision options — IR, colour, spotlight & siren (where supported)

IR night vision shows black-and-white in total darkness and is the most power-friendly. Colour night vision uses brighter sensors or built-in lights. Spotlight models actively light the area and can startle intruders; siren models can sound an alarm automatically or manually from the app. Availability is strictly per model.

Best for
Night-critical sites: gates, stores, fuel points, tool sheds, vehicle areas.
Network
Not network-dependent.
Power reality
Spotlights and colour modes raise power consumption noticeably — size the panel accordingly.
Storage
No change; night clips consume the same storage as day clips per minute.
Setup
Test the night image at the real mounting height before final fixing.
Failure points & what to ask before buying

Common issues: IR reflecting off nearby walls/insects causing white haze, spotlight draining battery in high-traffic areas, sirens annoying neighbours.

Ask: What is the manufacturer's night-vision range for this exact model? Does the spotlight stay on continuously or trigger with motion?

Ask about this type

11. Two-way audio cameras (where supported)

Speak through the camera from your phone and hear visitors — useful for telling delivery people where to leave goods, warning trespassers, or checking on farm workers. Audio quality and volume vary a lot between models.

Best for
House gates, shops, farm store rooms, sites with caretakers.
Network
Needs enough bandwidth for smooth audio — weak 4G can make speech choppy.
Power reality
Minor additional draw when active.
Storage
Whether audio is recorded alongside video depends on the model and local settings.
Setup
Microphone/speaker permissions in the app; a quick two-way test after install.
Failure points & what to ask before buying

Common issues: echo or delay, mic blocked by the housing, loud sites drowning out speech.

Ask: Does the model record audio too, or only live two-way talk? Is there a loudspeaker loud enough for outdoor use?

Ask about this type

12. Multi-camera and multi-site setups

One camera watching a gate rarely tells the whole story. Multiple solar cameras can be grouped in one app (many ecosystems support 2–30+ devices), while multi-site means, for example, a farmhouse and a Tumakuru shop both visible from the same phone. Larger installations may mix solar cameras with mains-powered NVR systems where power exists.

Best for
Farm boundaries, layouts, factories, schools, and families with two properties.
Network
Each camera needs its own connectivity (Wi-Fi or SIM); 4G setups mean one data plan per camera unless the model supports sharing — confirm per model.
Power reality
Total energy and data costs scale with camera count — plan each unit separately.
Storage
Per-camera cards, or central storage if a compatible recorder is part of the design.
Setup
Planning exercise first — the coverage worksheet helps; then staged installation.
Failure points & what to ask before buying

Common issues: app account limits, juggling many SIM plans, mixed brands that refuse to live in one app.

Ask: Can all chosen models be viewed in one app? What is the maximum number of devices per account? Who keeps the account ownership?

Ask about this type

13. Solar CCTV for farms & remote agricultural land

The classic Tumakuru-district use case: no grid power at the field, no Wi-Fi for kilometres, and assets worth protecting — crops, equipment, pump sets, harvest stores, and boundary encroachment. A 4G SIM solar camera on a tall pole, aimed along approaches and the store, covers most of what matters.

Best for
Open fields, orchards, farm houses, equipment sheds, borewell points.
Network
4G SIM if signal exists at the pole position — always test first; otherwise local-recording cameras reviewed by visit.
Power reality
Excellent sun exposure is the one advantage farms have — panels thrive here if kept clean and clear of shade.
Storage
microSD on camera; consider a second “decoy vs real” placement discussion during site assessment.
Setup
Pole or rooftop mount, theft-resistant fixings, panel tilted per manufacturer guidance.
Failure points & what to ask before buying

Common issues: camera or panel theft itself, cattle rubbing poles, dust on panels after harvest season, signal blocked by dense tree lines.

Ask: Where is the nearest point with confirmed 4G signal? Who can physically check the camera weekly? Is there a lockable structure to mount on?

Ask about farm solar CCTV

14. Solar systems for construction sites, yards, layouts, warehouses & temporary sites

Sites change daily, power comes and goes, and cable theft is its own risk — exactly where wire-free solar cameras earn their keep. They can move with the project phase: watch material storage this month, the boundary next month.

Best for
Building projects, material yards, plotting layouts, godowns, temporary event parking.
Network
4G SIM typically; Wi-Fi if the site office router reaches.
Power reality
Dust on panels is the big factor on construction sites — plan a weekly wipe-down.
Storage
microSD plus (if the model supports) cloud for off-site copies of critical areas.
Setup
Quick redeployment is the whole point — keep mounts generic and record each camera's location in the app.
Failure points & what to ask before buying

Common issues: cameras knocked by machinery, concrete dust on lenses, vibration misaligning the view, loss during site handover.

Ask: Who owns the cameras when the project ends? Is there a safe night position for each unit?

Ask about site solar CCTV

15. Hybrid arrangements — solar plus mains power or backup

Where dependable power reaches part of the property, the smart design often mixes technologies: mains-powered cameras or a recorder for the buildings, solar cameras for the far gate, pump house and boundary. Solar then covers the gaps instead of carrying the whole load — usually the most reliable and economical outcome.

Best for
Farmhouses with line power, factories, schools, apartments — buildings plus open ground.
Network
Router + Wi-Fi for buildings, 4G SIM units for remote corners.
Power reality
Hybrid designs keep working when one source fails; solar units carry the no-power zones.
Storage
NVR/central storage where power exists, microSD on remote solar units.
Setup
Highest planning effort — worth doing with a site assessment.
Failure points & what to ask before buying

Common issues: mixed-brand apps that don't combine, Wi-Fi dead zones between building and boundary, unclear ownership of the recording system.

Ask: Which areas have dependable power today? Where does the Wi-Fi actually end? What must be recorded even during a full power cut?

Questions about solar CCTV models

What is the difference between lenses, camera units and screens?

A lens is one imaging opening on a camera. A camera unit is one physical housing. A screen or view is one picture stream shown in the app. A dual-lens camera has two lenses in one unit, and a kit of two cameras has two units. A product can also show three screens from two lenses. This catalogue records each count in its own row and writes “Not verified” when the maker or listing does not say.

Does a 4G solar camera need Wi-Fi?

No. A 4G SIM model connects through a mobile data plan, so it needs a SIM with active data and mobile signal where it is mounted. Which operators and bands a given model supports is listed in its datasheet, and this page does not promise that every Indian SIM will work. A Wi-Fi solar camera needs a Wi-Fi network within reach.

Why do so many records say “Not verified”?

Most Indian listings give only a title and a few features. Where the maker, a retailer or a press report did not state a value, the catalogue leaves it blank instead of guessing. Every record lists which sources were used and what type of evidence each value comes from.

Are prices shown?

No. Prices change often and listing prices are not offers. Send a record's WhatsApp enquiry to +91 97432 74005 for current price, stock, installation and warranty.

Does Safe and Secure Solutions stock or hold an authorisation for these brands?

This catalogue makes no such claim. It is a research and enquiry aid. Availability, sourcing and installation are confirmed model by model after you enquire.

What should I check about BIS before buying an internet-connected camera?

Reuters reported on 1 October 2026 that most of about 770 internet camera listings it checked on Amazon India and Flipkart did not appear in the BIS database of approved models, and that the marketplaces had begun reviewing them. Ask the seller for the registration details of the exact maker and model, and compare them with the BIS portal. A certificate for one model or brand does not cover every other model.

How are the counts on this page produced?

They are calculated from the records in the catalogue itself, and the figures in the research summary table are written from that same data. They describe what was found in this research pass, not the whole Indian market.

The Six Things Every Solar CCTV Setup Must Get Right

Panel, battery, camera, SIM/network, app and storage — understand each one and you can judge any quotation or product page on your own. Exact values always come from the specific model's manual, which takes precedence over everything on this page.

☀️ Solar panel

  • Wattage must cover daily camera use with margin — size it, don't guess it.
  • Tilt and direction per the manufacturer's guidance; in India, generally facing south-ish with sun exposure through the day.
  • No shade, ever — a small shadow on one corner can cut output disproportionately.
  • Clean monthly; more often near roads, harvest dust or construction.

🔋 Battery

  • Capacity (Wh) decides night and cloudy-day endurance.
  • Batteries age — expect gradually reduced capacity over years; heat accelerates it.
  • Very cold mornings and very hot afternoons both reduce effective capacity.
  • Ask whether the battery is replaceable before you buy, not after it fails.

📷 Camera

  • Resolution is marketing until you see a real night sample — judge images, not numbers.
  • Lens/field of view decides area covered vs detail at distance.
  • IP rating indicates dust/water protection — check the rating for outdoor use in the datasheet.
  • Mounting height matters: typical guidance is around 2.5–3 m for entrances — high enough to avoid tamper, low enough to recognise faces.

📶 SIM / network

  • Confirm the camera supports your operator and LTE bands — from the manual, not from a shop's word.
  • Check SIM size, activation status and whether PIN lock must be off.
  • Choose a data plan matching the estimated usage.
  • APN settings only if the manufacturer requires them; test signal at the exact mount point.

📱 App

  • Install only the official app from a verified app-store listing or the maker's site.
  • Create the account with a unique strong password — never one reused from elsewhere.
  • Set timezone, alerts, and family access deliberately; share the minimum access needed.
  • Update firmware when the app offers it — never power off mid-update.

💾 Storage

  • microSD: private and cheap, but stolen with the camera — use endurance-rated cards.
  • Cloud: off-site copies, typically a recurring fee, only where the model officially supports it.
  • Recorder/NVR: central storage where power and wiring allow.
  • Check supported capacity, overwrite behaviour and retention days in the exact model's manual.

1. Before you buy — the 7-point verification

  1. Network: is there genuine Wi-Fi or 4G signal at the mount point (test at pole height)?

  1. Select the mounting point for view first, then check the panel sun at that spot.
  2. Avoid shade: tree lines grow, awnings appear — leave future margin.
  3. Point the panel per the manufacturer's guidance; secure both brackets firmly.
  4. Protect every connector; weatherproof the cable entry (drip loops, sealed glands).
  5. Aim the camera at the real target — gates and approaches, not sky or empty field.
  6. Check privacy: never aim into neighbours' private areas; note what your camera sees.

Will a solar CCTV camera work where there is no electricity at all?

Which SIM card works in a 4G solar camera?

How much mobile data does a solar camera use?

Anywhere from a few hundred megabytes to tens of gigabytes a month — driven by bitrate, how much you record, how much you watch live, and codec efficiency. How long does the battery last during cloudy or rainy days?

Battery capacity (Wh) divided by the camera's daily consumption gives autonomy days — e.g. a 40 Wh battery running a 5 W camera around 6 equivalent hours a day can ride out roughly a day without meaningful sun. Monsoon planning means honest margins: bigger battery, lower bitrate, motion-only recording. The checker shows the maths with your numbers.

Does it record 24/7 continuously, or only on motion?

Both types exist — but continuous recording on solar power demands large battery and panel capacity plus big storage, and not every model supports it. Most solar cameras record on motion by design, which suits farms and gates perfectly well.     Troubleshooting

    When Something Stops Working — Safe Checks Before You Panic

    Each problem below follows the same structure: symptom → safe checks → likely causes → when to call a technician. Never open the device, never work at height in rain, and never attempt repairs beyond cleaning and reseating external parts.

    Camera shows offline in the app

    Safe checks: confirm your phone has internet; check the camera's status in the app after 2–3 minutes; power-cycle nothing at height — check from ground what you can see (panel connector, obvious damage). Likely causes: network drop at the site, router reboot (Wi-Fi models), SIM data exhausted, camera battery fully drained. Call a technician when: the camera stays offline a full day with good weather and confirmed network — especially with no charging indication.

    No SIM network / “no service”

    Safe checks: test the same SIM in a phone at the site; verify the plan is active with data remaining; confirm the SIM is the correct size and seated properly (remove power first). Likely causes: inactive/expired plan, SIM not activated, operator not supported by the camera's bands, poor coverage at that spot. Call a technician when: the SIM works in a phone but not in the camera — that points to band/APN/hardware issues needing proper diagnosis.

    APN errors / data not working though signal shows

    Safe checks: confirm from the camera's manual whether it needs a specific APN; re-enter the APN exactly as the manufacturer documents; restart the camera after saving. Likely causes: wrong or missing APN, SIM provisioned for phone use only, carrier restriction. Call a technician when: the manual's exact APN still fails — carrier-side or hardware issues need a site visit.

    Weak signal / laggy live view

    Safe checks: read signal strength in the app at different times of day; try live view on strong phone data; for Wi-Fi, test beside the router vs at the camera spot. Likely causes: distance/walls (Wi-Fi), marginal coverage (4G), congestion at peak hours, antenna obstruction. Call a technician when: video is unusable daily — a repositioned mount, external antenna (if the model supports one), or a different connection type may be needed.

    App pairing / device binding fails

    Safe checks: confirm you installed the official app from the manual/box listing (not a look-alike); check the QR/device ID entry is exact; ensure the phone is on the correct network step the manual asks for; try the pairing with another phone before suspecting the camera. Likely causes: wrong app, wrong band (2.4 vs 5 GHz Wi-Fi), account region issues, camera already bound to another account. Call a technician when: two phones both fail — the unit may need service or proper re-binding.

    Camera not charging from solar

    Safe checks: look for charging status in the app on a sunny day; inspect the panel surface for dust, leaves or shade; check the panel cable and connector are firm and undamaged (from ground/step stool, safely). Likely causes: heavy shading, dirty panel, loose/damaged connector, failed panel or charge circuit. Call a technician when: a clean, sunny, firmly connected panel still shows no charge across a full day.

    Battery drains too fast

    Safe checks: review the app's battery graph; count motion events per day (busy roads trigger constantly); check whether continuous recording was enabled recently; note night temperatures. Likely causes: heavy traffic triggering recordings, continuous mode on an undersized system, ageing battery, cold mornings. Call a technician when: a quiet site with sensible settings still drains in hours — capacity or hardware assessment is due.

    Too many false alerts

    Safe checks: check what the camera sees — trees, flags, busy road edges, insect webs near the lens at night; reduce sensitivity; enable person/vehicle detection if the model supports it; set activity zones if available. Likely causes: foliage movement, headlights sweeping the lens, insects at night, over-sensitive defaults. Call a technician when: alerts remain unusable after tuning — a small re-aim often fixes what settings cannot.

    No night image / night view is poor

    Safe checks: compare night modes (IR vs colour) in the app; check for spider webs or dust on the lens and IR window; check whether nearby walls reflect IR into the lens (white haze); ensure the area has some reflective surfaces if using IR. Likely causes: obstructed IR window, IR reflection, distance beyond the model's real night range, no reflective targets in open fields. Call a technician when: the image stays dark or hazy after cleaning — night performance is a model/siting matter worth reviewing on site.

    No playback / recordings missing

    Safe checks: confirm storage exists and is formatted per the app; check recording mode was actually enabled; verify the date/time filter you are using; check card health status if the app reports it. Likely causes: recording disabled, card full/failing/counterfeit, wrong timezone confusion, overwrite settings. Call a technician when: a known-good endurance card still shows gaps — request a proper check before trusting the unit as evidence.

    Memory card errors

    Safe checks: back up wanted clips first; reformat the card in the app (manual's procedure); confirm the card meets the model's supported capacity and class; use only genuine, endurance-rated cards. Likely causes: card wear-out, counterfeit card, capacity beyond model support, dirty contacts (power off before reseating). Call a technician when: errors persist with a verified genuine card of supported size.

    Water ingress / fogging inside the lens

    Safe checks: from the ground, check the cable entry point and housing seals; confirm the unit is within its IP rating's realistic exposure; do not open the housing. Likely causes: cable entry not sealed, cracked housing, gasket failure, mounting where water runs onto the lens. Call a technician when: any fogging or droplets appear inside — internal moisture needs professional attention and may be warranty-relevant (keep the invoice).

    Intermittent connectivity — works, then drops

    Safe checks: note the times it drops (peak hours? after router reboots? during rain?); check for new obstructions — grown branches, parked vehicles, construction; read signal logs if the app keeps them. Likely causes: marginal signal that fades with weather/congestion, router DHCP changes, loose external connector, energy-saving sleep settings. Call a technician when: drops happen daily despite good signal readings — a systematic site look beats more guessing.

    Factory reset & firmware update cautions

    Factory reset erases your settings (pairing, schedules, and on some models recordings) — use it only after targeted troubleshooting fails, and follow the exact manual sequence for your model. Firmware updates must never be interrupted: start only with healthy battery charge and stable connection, and leave the camera powered throughout. If either step fails midway, stop and call rather than improvising — half-applied updates are the fastest route to a dead unit.

    Privacy & security guidance

    Keep Your Cameras Yours — Security That Actually Matters

    A security camera that is easy to hack protects nobody. These habits cost nothing and prevent the most common real-world failures of camera security.

    Account & device security

    • Strong, unique password for the camera app account — never reused from email or banking.
    • Official apps only, from the store listing named in the manual — look-alike apps steal credentials.
    • Update firmware when the app offers it — updates fix real security holes.
    • Share least privilege: give family/staff viewer access where possible, not full admin; remove access when people leave.
    • Secure the base: keep your router's Wi-Fi password strong (WPA2/WPA3) and don't disable its firewall.
    • Never share SIM PINs, camera passwords or device IDs on any website form — including ours; we will never ask for them.

    Responsible camera placement

    • Cover your property: gates, approaches, yards, stores — not neighbours' homes, windows or private space.
    • If a camera unavoidably overlooks a shared area, tell the people affected — transparency prevents disputes.
    • Never place cameras where people expect complete privacy (bathrooms, changing areas) — full stop.
    • For schools/workplaces, keep footage access restricted to authorised staff and state the purpose clearly.
    • Recordings may capture neighbours' comings and goings incidentally — treat that footage as sensitive and don't publish it.

    This is practical guidance, not legal advice — applicable privacy rules depend on your context and jurisdiction.

    Warranty & maintenance

    Warranty, Service & Long-Term Care — Stated Plainly

    We do not manufacture cameras, so we make no independent warranty promises. What we do: verify warranty terms with you at purchase, coordinate service per the manufacturer's process, and keep your system maintained so warranty claims rarely become necessary.

    Warranty facts

    • Warranty is the manufacturer's, and varies by brand and model — confirmed in writing on your invoice.
    • Typical exclusions include physical damage, water ingress beyond rating, power surges and unauthorised repairs — read your warranty card.
    • Keep the invoice and warranty card; photograph the serial number on installation day.
    • We state warranty terms as per the manufacturer's documents only — never from memory or marketing talk.

    Routine maintenance

    • Monthly: wipe the panel; glance at the app's battery and signal status.
    • Quarterly: clean the lens cover; check mounts, connectors and cable entry; verify recordings exist.
    • After storms: inspect brackets and panel angle; confirm the camera reconnected itself.
    • Yearly: review storage health, replace ageing microSD cards proactively, review who has app access.

    Service & support

    • Troubleshooting help on WhatsApp with photos of the app screen — usually the fastest path.
    • Service visits confirmed case by case across our service area.
    • Optional periodic maintenance visits — enquire for terms.
    • Battery ageing is normal: expect gradual capacity decline over years; replacement is a normal service, not a defect.
